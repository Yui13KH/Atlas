## Rule 1 — No AI during first attempts

If you're given:

No ChatGPT.

Draw the problem.

Write pseudocode.

Think.

# Rule 2 — AI is your reviewer

Your proposed use is basically perfect.

You can come to me with:

> "Here's what I learned about pointers."

> "Here's my explanation of RAII."

> "Here's the implementation I wrote."

> "Here's what I think happens in memory."

> "Here are the tests I wrote."

And ask:

> **"Critique this. What am I misunderstanding? What subtle things am I missing? Is my mental model correct?"**

That's completely different from:

> "Teach me pointers."

or:

> "Write a vector class."

The first makes **you produce the knowledge**.

The second lets the AI produce it for you.

---

# Rule 3 — Explain before looking anything up

This is extremely important.

Suppose you're learning:

**references vs pointers.**

Before reading another explanation, close the book.

Write:

> "A reference is..."

Then explain it.

Then test yourself:

> "What happens when..."

Only **after that** should you compare your understanding with a trusted source.

That's how you discover gaps.

---

# Rule 4 — Every major topic needs an artifact

Don't just consume information.

For every significant topic:

**Learn → explain → implement → test → reflect**

For example:

### RAII

Learn it.

Explain it in your own words.

Implement a resource-owning class.

Break it deliberately.

Fix it.

Write down what you learned.

That's vastly more valuable than watching three hours of C++ videos.
