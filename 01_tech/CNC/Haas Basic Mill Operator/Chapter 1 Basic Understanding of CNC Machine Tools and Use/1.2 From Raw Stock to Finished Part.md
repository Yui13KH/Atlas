
# Raw Material Processing & CNC Machining Execution: Technical Notes

### 1. Material Logistics and Pre-Machining Preparation

Before any raw metal touches a CNC mill, it undergoes a controlled preparation and verification pipeline to transition from bulk inventory into individual blanks.

- **Stock Selection:** Machine shops store raw material (aluminum, steel, brass) in standardized, long bar stock lengths. Rectangular parts begin with matching rectangular bar profiles.
    
- **Primary Cutting:** Bulk bars are fed via rollers into a industrial **bandsaw** to be sectioned into short lengths ("blanks") required for the specific job run.
    
- **Inventory Tracking:** Cut blanks are counted and tagged (via barcodes or routing sheets) to maintain traceability between the warehouse and the shop floor.
    
- **Documentation Matching:** Before loading, the operator verifies that the **Part Number** matches across three key documents:
    
    1. The **Route Sheet** (Shop traveler / Job order)
        
    2. The **Engineering Drawing** (Blueprints establishing geometric tolerances)
        
    3. The **Physical Material Batch**
        
- **Dimensional Sanity Check:** A simple check with a tape measure confirms the blank's raw stock dimensions match the print before committing machine time.
    

### 2. Machine Setup & Offset Registration

Setup prepares the machine cell to execute a specific part file predictably and accurately.

- **Workholding Installation:** Mounting a precision vise to the T-slot table, tramming it parallel/perpendicular to the machine's axes, and securing it.
    
- **Physical Part Stop:** Installing a fixed mechanical reference arm (stop) inside or beside the vise jaws. This ensures every raw blank sits in the exact same physical position along the X-axis.
    
- **Tool Height Offsets (Probing):** Measuring and recording the length each tool extends from the spindle face. Modern CNCs use a table-mounted **tool probe** (a touch sensor) to automate this process; older systems require manual touch-offs.
    
- **Work Offsets ($G54$):** Locating the exact physical coordinates of the workpiece relative to the machine's home position using a **work probe** (or edge finder) to touch the blank's reference faces.
    
- **Program Transfer:** Loading the verified G-code file into the controller via USB or Local Area Network (Ethernet/Wi-Fi). Program headers usually contain comment fields matching the blueprint number.
    

### 3. Production Cycle Execution & Operator Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

```
                       [ LOAD & ALIGN BLANK ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                     [ TIGHTEN VISE & CLOSE DOOR ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                         [ CYCLE START ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                   [ ACOUSTIC & VISUAL MONITOR ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
                 [ UNLOAD, AIR-BLOW PART & VISE ]
                                 │
                                 ▼
             [ PART QUALITY CONTROL & TOLERANCE CHECK ]
```

**The Machining Loop:**

1. **Load:** Insert the raw blank into the vise, referencing it against the part stop and ensuring it sits square against the jaws.
    
2. **Secure:** Torque the vise to prevent part movement under heavy cutting loads, close the enclosure doors, and hit **Cycle Start**.
    
3. **Monitor:** On the **First Article** (the initial part of a run), the operator watches the cutting operations closely and listens for chatter, screeching, or erratic vibrations.
    
4. **Intervention:** If an anomaly occurs, hitting **Feed Hold** halts tool movement while keeping the spindle rotating, preserving the cutting tool and preventing hard crashes.
    

**Post-Cycle Handling & Quality Control:**

- **Clean Down:** Open doors, loosen vise, and immediately clear chips and coolant off the finished part and vise locating surfaces using a **compressed airgun**.
    
- **Off-Line Inspection:** While the next part is running, inspect the newly machined part using precision tools (calipers, micrometers).
    
- **Non-Conformance Management:** Parts out of tolerance are isolated immediately to prevent contamination of the good batch, and the machine is paused after the active cycle to resolve tool wear or offset drift.
    

### 4. Tooling & Feature Geometry

|**Tool Type**|**Primary Function**|**Features Created**|
|---|---|---|
|**Face Mill**|High-rate material removal across large surface areas.|Creates flat top reference planes and sets overall part height.|
|**End Mill**|Periphery cutting, slotting, and cavity milling.|Profiles outer geometry, steps, pockets, and counterbores.|
|**Drill Bit**|Axially plunge-cutting round holes.|Creates through-holes, blind holes, and pilot holes for tapping.|
|**Tap**|Forming internal helical threads.|Cuts machine threads into pre-drilled holes (requires specific tap-drill diameters).|
