# Shop Materials & Stock Types: Quick Reference Guide for New Operators

## 1. The Four Common Metals

|**Material**|**Key Characteristics & Behavior**|**Machining Notes**|
|---|---|---|
|**Mild Steel**|• Most common shop material.<br><br>  <br><br>• Soft, easy to form/weld, magnetic.<br><br>  <br><br>• Can be "gummy" (leaving rough surfaces if milled wrong).<br><br>  <br><br>• Rushes easily (needs rust preventative).|Very versatile; can use a wide range of cutting tools (HSS or carbide).|
|**Aluminum**|• Weighs ~35% as much as steel; non-magnetic; rust-resistant.<br><br>  <br><br>• 6061 is the most ubiquitous alloy.<br><br>  <br><br>• Very common in aerospace.|Cut 2–3x faster than soft steel. **Requires 2–3 flute tools** and steady coolant to prevent gumming/clogging.|
|**Alloy Steels**|• Tougher, harder, and stronger than mild steel (e.g., chromoly, tool steels).<br><br>  <br><br>• Magnetic.<br><br>  <br><br>• Used for high-performance parts, dies, and cutting tools.|Requires **high-end carbide tools** and carefully selected parameters due to high toughness.|
|**Stainless Steel**|• Non-magnetic, corrosion-resistant, used heavily in medical/food industries.<br><br>  <br><br>• Tough and wear-resistant.|**Difficult to machine.** Requires slow speeds/feeds (often ~3x slower than mild steel) and specialized carbide/coated tools. Avoid high-speed steel (HSS).|

## 2. Preformed Stock Shapes

Raw stock is manufactured into standard shapes before being cut down to match the approximate outside dimensions of the final part:

- **Sheet Stock:** Thickness under $1/4$". Used mostly for sheet metal fabrication, laser/plasma cutting, or stamping.
    
- **Plate Stock:** Thickness from $1/4$" to over $1$". Too thick for sheet processes; cut using saws, torches, or waterjets.
    
    - _Hot Rolled (HR):_ Rolled at $1700\degree\text{F}+$; has a crusty scale, less dimensional accuracy, but easy to form.
        
    - _Cold Rolled (CR):_ Further processed at lower temperatures; smoother surface, dark gray, tighter dimensional tolerances.
        
- **Square & Rectangular Bar Stock:** Standard lengths ($8$, $10$, $12$ ft) used to machine solid blocks without wasting massive chunks of plate.
    
- **Round Bar Stock:** Diameters from $1/4$" to $12$"+. Turned or cut primarily on lathes/turning centers. Ranges from rough hot-rolled bar to precision-ground shafting.
    
- **Tubular Stock:** Square or rectangular tubes often machined (for bolted connections/notches) prior to welding structures.
    

## 3. Material Identification & Best Practices

- **Quick Magnet Test:**
    
    - _Mild & Alloy Steels:_ Magnetic (magnets stick firmly).
        
    - _Stainless Steel:_ Generally non-magnetic.
        
    - _Aluminum:_ Never magnetic.
        
- **Identification:** Raw stock is usually tagged or labeled with a Sharpie/paint pen by the purchaser. **Always verify the material matches the print/blueprint.** Using the wrong material is like using the wrong recipe—the part will fail.
    
- **Tool Matching:** Every material requires specific cutting tools, geometries, and speeds. Changing materials (e.g., switching from steel to aluminum) means changing your tooling setup.
