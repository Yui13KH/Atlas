	# CNC Mill Cutting Tools: Quick Reference Guide for New Operators

## 1. Drilling Tools

- **Twist Drills:** The most common tool for making holes. Features two cutting edges and flutes that force chips out of the hole.
    
- **Indexable Drills:** Used for larger-diameter holes. Instead of replacing the whole drill, you simply rotate or replace the **replaceable cutting inserts** when they get dull.
    

## 2. Milling Cutters (End Mills & Face Mills)

- **End Mills:** Rotate on their center axis and primarily cut with the **side** of the tool (parallel to the axis of rotation).
    
    - _Square End Mills:_ Sharp 90-degree corners.
        
    - _Bullnose End Mills:_ Feature radii on the corners to prevent chipping.
        
    - _Ball Nose End Mills:_ Spherical ends used for 3D contours and curved shapes.
        
    - _Chamfer Mills / Corner Chamfers:_ 45-degree angles on the corners or tips to break sharp edges.
        
- **Face Mills:** Cut with the face of the tool (perpendicular to the axis of rotation), primarily used to flatten or finish the top surface of a part.
    
- **Roughing End Mills ("Corncob" Mills):** Have wavy slots/grooves on the cutting edges. Used for heavy material removal because they break chips into smaller pieces and lower required horsepower. Do not leave a good surface finish.
    
- **Finishing End Mills:** Typically have **5 or more flutes** with small gullets (gaps for chips). Designed for excellent surface finishes in hardened steels, but cannot take aggressive roughing cuts.
    

## 3. Threading Tools

- **Taps:** Used to cut internal threads in drilled holes. Requires the hole to be pre-drilled to a specific size.
    
    - _Spiral Flute Taps:_ Pull chips **up and out** of the hole. Best for **blind holes** (holes that do not go all the way through).
        
    - _Straight Flute Taps:_ Push chips **down**. Best for **through holes** (holes that go all the way through).
        
- **Thread Mills:** Used to cut external threads or large internal threads via programmed helical interpolation.
    

## 4. Material-Specific Design (Aluminum vs. Steel)

- **For Aluminum:** Use end mills with **2 or 3 flutes**. They have very large gullets to handle the high volume of chips produced by soft materials.
    
- **For Steels (Mild, Alloy, Stainless):** Use end mills with **4 or more flutes**.
    
    - _Rule of Thumb:_ 2-3 flute tools are for aluminum; 4+ flute tools are for steel. 4+ flute tools are "spectacularly bad" at cutting aluminum.
