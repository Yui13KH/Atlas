

# Haas CNC Machine: Part Loading and Unloading Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

This comprehensive guide covers the essential techniques, workholding concepts, and step-by-step procedures for loading and unloading workpieces in a CNC milling machine based on Haas certification standards.

## 1. Fundamentals of CNC Workholding & Locating

You cannot simply place a raw workpiece on the table; the cutting forces will throw it. Workpieces are held in fixtures that align the part across the three basic milling axes ($X$, $Y$, and $Z$).

### The 3-Axis Locating Rule

For precise machining, every part must be positioned consistently in the same location along each axis:

- **$X$-Axis:** Positioned using a **part stop**.
    
- **$Y$-Axis:** Positioned against the **stationary jaw** of the vise.
    
- **$Z$-Axis:** Positioned by resting on the **bottom of the vise** or on precision **parallels**.
    

## 2. Common Types of Jaws & Fixtures

### Mill Vise Options

- **Standard Hard Jaws:** Made of hardened steel to provide an accurate, wear-resistant locating surface. Used for clamping standard rectangular raw stock.
    
- **Parallels:** Precision ground bars placed under the part in hard jaws to elevate it so a specific amount sits above the jaws for cutting.
    
- **Step Jaws:** Jaws with a notch cut into them where the part sits directly on the ledge and gets clamped.
    
- **Nested / Soft Jaws (2nd Operation):** Jaws machined out of softer material to match the unique, irregular profile of a part that has already undergone first-operation machining. The custom pocket forces the part to self-locate in $X$ and $Y$ without needing a separate part stop.
    
- **Dual-Station Vise:** Features a stationary center section and two movable outer jaws operated by a single screw. Allows machining two parts at once, or running Operation 1 on one side and Operation 2 on the other simultaneously.
    

### Specialized Fixtures

Used for specific, non-standard parts based on shape and material:

- **Tombstone Fixtures**
    
- **Window Fixtures**
    
- _Core Rule:_ All fixtures—regardless of complexity—must feature a **repeatable locating method** and a **rigid clamping mechanism**.
    

## 3. Step-by-Step Loading & Unloading Procedure

### Step 1: Pre-Unload In-Machine Cleaning

1. After the machine cycle finishes, use an air gun to clean chips and coolant off the workpiece and fixture before unclamping.
    
2. **Technique:** Start at the highest point of the part/fixture and use a sweeping side-to-side motion working downward.
    
3. **Hex Screw Maintenance:** Thoroughly blow out socket head cap screws or fixture clamps; accumulated chips in hex heads prevent wrenches from seating fully, leading to stripped tools.
    
4. **Safety Warning (Coolant Spray):** Pockets and blind holes trap liquid that can blow directly back at you. Hide behind the enclosure door, extending only your arm and air gun inside during the initial blowout. Alternatively, wait to unclamp the part and manually tilt it to dump trapped coolant.
    

### Step 2: Unload the Part

1. Loosen the vise or fixture clamps.
    
2. Lift the part out. If needed, hold it with your hand and use the air gun to blow off remaining coolant/chips from all sides.
    
3. Place the finished/semi-finished part in its designated measuring/inspection area.
    

### Step 3: Thoroughly Clean Locating Surfaces

1. Inspect and blow clean all locating pads, vise beds, step jaws, and stops.
    
2. **Preventing Scrap & Damage:**
    
    - A single chip trapped between the part and locating surface can tilt/cock the workpiece, putting all machined features out of tolerance or causing a bad surface finish.
        
    - Clamping a part on top of trapped debris can permanently dent/ding the precision-ground locating surface, marring every subsequent part loaded into the machine.
        

### Step 4: Load New Workpiece

1. Set the raw stock down onto the $Z$-axis locating surface (vise bottom or parallels).
    
2. Push the part simultaneously against the $X$-axis stop and $Y$-axis stationary jaw.
    
3. While applying light manual pressure toward the stops, lightly snug the vise or clamp.
    
4. **Special Handling:**
    
    - **Thin-Walled Parts:** Use a torque wrench or lighter pressure as directed to avoid bending or deforming the part.
        
    - **Seating the Part:** Tap the top of the part with a dead-blow mallet (if specified) to ensure it sits flat against the $Z$-locating surface before final torque.
        

### Step 5: Verify Part Position

1. Visually check all contact points to verify the part is seated fully against all locating surfaces.
    
2. Use a thin piece of **shim stock** (feeler gauge) under the part to test for gaps between the workpiece and locating surfaces if required by quality control.
    

### Step 6: Cycle Execution & Active Monitoring

1. Close the enclosure doors.
    
2. Position hands with **one thumb on `CYCLE START`** and the **other thumb on `FEED HOLD`**.
    
3. **NEVER turn your back on the machine after pressing start**, especially on new setups or the first 5–10 parts of a production run.
    
4. Keep your thumb on `FEED HOLD` in anticipation of an issue until the first cutting tool engages the material and visually/audibly cuts smoothly.
