# CNC Workholding & Part Loading Protocol

## 1. The Hydraulic Chuck

Most CNC lathes use a 3-jaw hydraulic chuck. Unlike a manual chuck, the jaws move via a hydraulic actuator at the rear of the spindle.

- **Clamping Logic:** Jaws can clamp **OD** (Outside Diameter) or **ID** (Inside Diameter for hollow parts).
    
- **Operation:** Use the foot pedal to trigger the clamp. This keeps both hands free to seat the part.
    
- **Seating:** Always push the part firmly against the back of the jaws (or the "stops") before clamping to ensure it is square.
    

### The Stroke Indicator (Your Question)

> **Question:** _If the part sizes aren't the same, what if the piece is bigger?_
> 
> **Answer:** The indicator doesn't show the _size_ of the part; it shows the **available travel (stroke)** of the hydraulic piston.

- Hydraulic jaws only move about **6mm – 12mm** total.
    
- If the part is too big or too small for where the jaws are currently bolted, the indicator will hit the "limit" (the end of the line).
    
- **The Goal:** You want the indicator in the **middle** of the stroke so that the machine has enough "room" to apply full hydraulic pressure. If it’s at the very end, it might _touch_ the part but not actually _squeeze_ it.

## 2. Hard Jaws vs. Soft Jaws

|**Feature**|**Hard Jaws**|**Soft Jaws**|
|---|---|---|
|**Material**|Hardened Steel (serrated "teeth")|Mild Steel or Aluminum (smooth)|
|**Use Case**|First operation on raw, rough stock.|Second operation on already machined surfaces.|
|**Grip**|"Bites" into the metal for high torque.|Matches the part's radius to prevent marking.|
|**Customization**|Permanent shape.|**Bored out** on the lathe to match the specific part diameter.|

## 3. The Collet Chuck

- **Design:** A sleeve with longitudinal slits that collapses around the part.
    
- **Benefit:** 360-degree contact. It provides much higher concentricity (center-alignment) and is faster for "bar work" (long rods) where you are making hundreds of small parts.
    

## 4. Standard Loading Sequence (SOP)

1. **Safety First:** Open the door and engage the "Setup" mode if necessary.
    
2. **Cleanliness:** Use the air gun to blow chips off the chuck faces and the workpiece. **A single chip trapped between the part and the jaw will make your part "wobble" (run-out).**
    
3. **The Foot Pedal:** Grasp the part, step on the pedal to release, swap the part, and step again to clamp.
    
4. **Verification:** Check the stroke indicator. If it's not in the safe zone, stop and adjust the jaw positions.
    

## 5. The "Golden Rule" of Manual Lathes

> **NEVER leave the chuck key in the chuck.**

- **The Physics:** If you turn on the spindle with the key inside, it becomes a projectile moving at several hundred feet per second. It can kill the operator or anyone standing nearby. **Hand on the key, or key in the rack—never in between.**
