
# CNC Milling & Production Workflow: Technical Study Notes

### 1. The Setup Phase (Preparation)
"Setup" is the critical transition from an idle machine to a production-ready cell. 
* **Definition:** A machine is "Set Up" once the specific workholding (vise/fixtures) is installed, tools are loaded/offset, and the coolant system is verified.
* **The "Source Code":** Once the physical setup is complete, the machine requires a **CNC Program** (G-Code) to define the toolpaths and coordinate movements.
* **Material Validation:** Never assume the stock is correct. You must verify the **basic shape** and **overall size** of the raw material (e.g., Rectangular Stock for a rectangular part) before the first cycle.

### 2. Operational Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)
The sequence of loading parts is a high-repetition task where consistency prevents "crashes."

**The Loading Loop:**
1. **Load:** Insert the raw blank into the vise.
2. **Seating:** Ensure the part is correctly seated (often using a dead blow hammer or checking for gaps against parallels).
3. **Secure:** Tighten the vise to ensure the part doesn't move under high cutting forces.
4. **Initiate:** Close the safety doors and hit **Cycle Start**.

**Post-Process Extraction:**
* Once the cycle ends: Open doors -> Loosen vise -> **Immediately blow the part off with an airgun**. This removes chips and coolant so you can inspect the part or remove it without making a mess.

### 3. Tooling Geometry and Function
In a CNC Mill, tools are categorized by the type of "feature" they create.

* **Face Mill:** The "Heavy Lifter." Its primary job is to create a perfectly flat top surface (Facing) to prepare the workpiece for further machining.
* **Endmill:** The "Architect." Used for **profiling** (cutting the outside shape) and **pocketing** (carving out internal cavities).
* **Drill & Tap Duo:** * The **Drill** creates the hole.
    * The **Tap** follows the drill to cut internal threads. 
    * *Technical Note:* The drill bit must be slightly smaller than the tap size (the "Tap Drill Size") to leave enough material for the threads.

### 4. Quality Control and Safety
* **The "First Article" Inspection:** The operator **must** watch the entire machining process of the very first part. This is the only way to catch "ghost" errors in the program before they ruin a batch.
* **Acoustic Monitoring:** Machinists develop "ear" for the machine.
    * **Strange Noises:** If you hear chatter, screeching, or unexpected impacts, hit **Feed Hold** immediately. This stops the motion but keeps the spindle turning, which is often safer than a hard Emergency Stop (E-Stop) which can break tools.
* **Production Advantage:** The primary benefit of CNC is **Repeatability**. Once the program and setup are "proven," you can produce identical parts with high precision indefinitely.
