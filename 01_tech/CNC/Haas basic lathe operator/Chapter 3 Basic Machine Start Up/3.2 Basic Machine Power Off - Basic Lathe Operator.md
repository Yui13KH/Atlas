# End-of-Shift & Weekend Maintenance Protocol

## 1. Professional Housekeeping (The "Clean Machine" Rule)

- **Chip Removal:** Ensure the conveyor belt is running until the internal tray is clear and the external bin is ready for the next shift.
    
- **Surface Cleaning:** Use a brush or vacuum for large clumps.
    
    - **Air Gun Safety:** When using compressed air to blow off the table, **use the machine door as a shield**. Keep it cracked just enough for the nozzle. This prevents "chip spray" from hitting you or your coworkers. Always maintain eye protection.
        
- **Work Area:** Wipe down tool benches, put away gauges/micrometers, and clean any oil or coolant spills on the floor immediately.
    

## 2. Coolant Management & The Refractometer

Coolant isn't just "water"; it’s a chemical balance that prevents rust and bacterial growth (which causes that "rotten egg" smell).

- **Level Check:** Ensure the sump is topped off so the next operator can start immediately.
    
- **Concentration Test (Refractometer):**
    
    1. Place 2–3 drops of coolant on the refractometer prism.
        
    2. Close the cover flap and look through the eyepiece toward a light source.
        
    3. **The Reading:** You’ll see a line between blue and white. Multiply this "Brix" number by your coolant’s specific factor (usually found on the barrel) to get your percentage (e.g., 7%–10% is standard).
        
    4. _Note:_ If the line is "fuzzy," it means there is **tramp oil** (leaked hydraulic/way oil) in the mix, which needs to be skimmed.
        

## 3. Weekend & Long-Term Shutdown

If the machine will sit idle for more than 48 hours, physics (moisture/oxidation) starts working against you.

- **Rust Prevention:**
    
    - Spray the table, chuck, and unpainted "ways" with a dedicated rust preventative (like WD-40 Specialist or a shop-specific oil).
        
    - Some modern coolants have "in-sump" inhibitors, but manual spraying is the only way to be 100% safe.
        
- **Axis Positioning:** Move the table/turret to the center of its travel. This prevents the "way covers" from being fully stretched or compressed, which preserves the seals.
    
- **Energy Isolation:**
    
    1. Turn off the control power.
        
    2. Shut down the main circuit breaker at the rear.
        
    3. **Air Supply:** Close the local air valve to prevent the compressor from cycling all weekend if there's a tiny leak.
        

## 4. Safety & Ethics

- **The E-Stop "Park":** If you need to reach deep into the machine to clear a "bird's nest" of chips, **engage the E-Stop**. This ensures the machine cannot move even if there is a software glitch or a button is bumped.
    
- **Gloves:** As you noted, use safety gloves for cleaning chips, but **remove them** the second you go back to operating or touching rotating parts.
