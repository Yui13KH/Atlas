# 📜 Chapter 7.1: Blueprint Reading & Tolerance Logic

### 1. Orthographic Projections: 1st vs. 3rd Angle

- **3rd Angle Projection (Standard in USA/Turkey):** * **Logic:** The "Glass Box" method. You are looking at the part from the **outside**.
    
    - **Layout:** The **Right** view is on the **Right** side of the Front view.
        
- **1st Angle Projection (Common in Europe/ISO):** * **Logic:** Your "First-Person" perspective. You are looking **through** the part.
    
    - **Layout:** The **Right** view is projected onto the **Left** side.
        

### 2. Specialized Drawing Views

- **Section View:** A "slice" through the part (usually indicated by a dashed line labeled `A-A`). It reveals internal features like bores, threads, and internal shoulders that are hidden from the outside.
    
- **Detail View:** A "magnifying glass" on a specific area. It’s a separate, larger circle used to show tiny chamfers or complex thread reliefs that would be too small to see in the main scale.
    

### 3. Symbols & Notation

- **⌀ (Diameter):** The most common symbol on a Lathe.
    
- **↧ (Depth):** Crucial for holes and bores.
    
- **R (Radius):** Often used for fillets and corner rounds.
    

### 4. The Developer’s Guide to Tolerances

In programming, a variable is either right or wrong. In machining, **nothing is perfect.** We use "Tolerances" to define the acceptable "range of error."

- **Decimal Precision Rule:** The number of digits after the decimal point tells you the "Priority Level" of the dimension.
    
    - `X.X` (± 0.1): Low priority (e.g., the length of a raw bar).
        
    - `X.XX` (± 0.01): Standard machining.
        
    - `X.XXX` (± 0.001): **Critical.** This is often a "press-fit" where two parts must slide together perfectly.
        
- **Tolerance Formats:**
    
    - **Bilateral:** `50.00 ± 0.02` (Range: 49.98 to 50.02).
        
    - **Unilateral:** `50.00 +0.02 / -0.00` (You can be bigger, but never smaller).
        
    - **Limit:** `50.02 / 49.98` (The drawing gives you the Max and Min directly).
        

### 5. "In Print" vs. "Scrap"

- **In Print:** The part measures anywhere within the allowed tolerance. Even if it’s `50.019`, it is technically "perfect" according to the contract.
    
- **Out of Tolerance:** Even `0.0001` outside the line makes the part a "paperweight" (scrap).
    

---

### 💡 Perfectionist Note:

As a C++ dev, you might want to hit the **nominal** (exact center) every time. While that's the goal, in the shop, "Time = Money." If a tolerance is wide (like `± 0.5`), don't waste 20 minutes trying to get it to `0.001`. **Speed-run the non-critical dimensions and focus your "CPU power" on the critical ones.**
