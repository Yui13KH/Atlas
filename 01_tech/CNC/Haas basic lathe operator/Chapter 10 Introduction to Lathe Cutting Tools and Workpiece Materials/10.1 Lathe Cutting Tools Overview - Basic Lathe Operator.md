# 🛠️ Chapter 9.1: Lathe Tooling & Insert Logic

### 1. The Core Concepts: O.D. vs. I.D.

- **O.D. (Outside Diameter):** Tools that cut the exterior of the part.
    
- **I.D. (Inside Diameter):** Tools that cut the interior.
    
    - **Boring Bars:** The technical name for I.D. turning tools. They require a pre-drilled hole to enter the part.
        

### 2. The Carbide Insert System

Modern CNC lathes use **indexable carbide inserts**.

- **Economy:** When one edge is dull or chipped, you don't throw the tool away; you "index" (rotate) the insert to a fresh edge.
    
- **Chip Breakers:** The "wrinkles" on top of the insert. They are designed to snap the metal "chips" into small 6-shaped curls.
    
    - _Warning:_ Without a chip breaker, you get "Bird's Nests" (long, stringy wires) that can wrap around the chuck and become a spinning saw.
        

### 3. Insert Shapes & Strengths

The angle of the diamond determines the trade-off between **strength** and **access**.

|**Shape**|**Angle**|**Application**|**Logic**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**80° Diamond** (CNMG/WNMG)|Large/Strong|**Roughing**|More "meat" behind the edge to handle heavy heat and force.|
|**55° Diamond** (DNMG)|Medium|General Purpose|Balanced strength and access.|
|**35° Diamond** (VNMG)|Sharp/Slim|**Finishing / Undercuts**|Can reach into tight grooves, but the tip is fragile.|

### 4. The Nose Radius ($TNRC$)

- **Large Radius:** Used for roughing. Distributes heat better and lasts longer.
    
- **Small Radius:** Used for finishing. Cuts "sharper" with less pressure, which prevents the part from flexing (deflection).
    
- **Center Line Rule:** The tip of the tool **must** be exactly on the spindle's center line.
    
    - If it's **too high**, the tool rubs and won't cut.
        
    - If it's **too low**, it leaves a "nub" or "tit" in the center of the part and can snap the insert.
        

### 5. Specialized Tooling

- **Grooving Tools:** Designed to cut deep narrow slots.
    
    - **Face Grooving:** Cuts a circle into the _front_ face of the part (like an O-ring groove).
        
- **Part-Off (Cut-off) Tool:** A very thin blade used to "drop" the finished part from the raw bar stock.
    
- **Threading Tools:**
    
    - **Non-Topping:** A general 60° point. It cuts the thread but doesn't touch the outer diameter ($OD$).
        
    - **Full Profile (Topping):** Specifically sized for one pitch (e.g., $1.5\text{mm}$). It cleans the "crest" (top) of the thread as it cuts, making it much more accurate.
        

### 6. Drills on a Lathe

On a Lathe, the **drill is stationary** and the **part rotates**.

- **Indexable Drills:** Uses carbide inserts instead of a twisted flute.
    
    - _Advantage:_ You can run them much faster than HSS (High Speed Steel) drills and you don't have to "peck" as much.

### 💡 Programmer's Pro-Tip:

When you are in your CAM software (like Fusion 360), pay close attention to the **Hand of Tool**.

- **Right-Hand Tool:** Moves from the Tailstock _toward_ the Spindle (standard).
    
- **Left-Hand Tool:** Moves from the Spindle _toward_ the Tailstock.
    
    If you select the wrong one in your code, the turret will try to move in a way that might cause a collision.
