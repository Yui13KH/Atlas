# 🌀 Chapter 6.2: Lathe-Specific Controls & Safety Logic

### 1. The Lathe "Measurement" Keys

On a Mill, you set a "Part Zero." On a Lathe, the geometry is cylindrical, so the buttons reflect that:

- **X Diameter Measure:** Tells the machine the current tool is touching a specific diameter.
    
- **Z Face Measure:** Tells the machine the tool is touching the "front" of the part ($Z0$).
    
- **X/Z Toggle:** Quickly switches the jog wheel control between the two primary axes.
    

### 2. Turret Management (The Tool Carousel)

- **Turret Fwd / Turret Rev:** Rotates the turret to the next/previous tool.
    
- **⚠️ Collision Warning:** Lathe tools stick out physically at different lengths. **NEVER** index (rotate) the turret when it is close to the spindle or the tailstock.
    
    - _Developer Logic:_ Unlike a Mill (where the ATC swaps tools in a safe spot), a Lathe turret rotates _everything_ in its current position. If Tool 1 is short and Tool 2 is a long drill, rotating the turret could swing that drill right into the chuck.
        

### 3. The Tailstock (TS)

The Tailstock is the "support" for long parts to prevent them from vibrating or flying out.

- **TS $\leftarrow$ / TS $\rightarrow$:** Moves the hydraulic tailstock toward or away from the spindle.
    
- **TS Rapid:** Increases travel speed.
    
- **Physics Note:** If your part length is $> 3x$ its diameter, you **must** use the tailstock to prevent the "missile" effect you calculated earlier.
    

### 4. Safety: The E-Stop vs. Feed Hold Protocol

This is the most important distinction for an operator.

|**Action**|**Result**|**Use Case**|
|---|---|---|
|**Feed Hold**|Pauses Axis motion; **Spindle stays spinning.**|Bad noise, suspected minor error, or checking a dimension. Saves the tool tip.|
|**E-Stop**|Kills **All** power (Axis + Spindle).|**Imminent Crash** or someone is in danger.|

- **The "Trenching" Risk:** If you E-Stop while a tool is mid-cut, the spindle stops instantly, but the tool is still embedded in the metal. Because the turret has a tiny bit of "play," the tool can "dig in" and snap the carbide tip.
    
- **The "Post-E-Stop" Trap:** E-Stop resets the program pointer to line 1. If you just clear the E-Stop and hit Cycle Start, the machine might try to move to its "Start Position" **through** the part.
    
    - **Protocol:** After E-Stop, always `Zero Return` or manually jog the turret to a "Safe Zone" ($X+$ and $Z+$) before restarting.
        

### 5. Operating Mindset

- **The Hand Rule:** As a beginner, one hand stays on **Feed Hold** and the other stays on the **Override 5%** button.
    
- **Ear Training:** You will eventually "code" with your ears. A "whistle" means the speed is too high; a "growl" means the feed is too heavy.
    
- **The "Reset" Mirage:** Pressing `Reset` clears the alarm, but it doesn't fix the physical cause. Always investigate the **Alarm Menu** (`Alarms` -> `History`) to see exactly what triggered the fail.
    

### 6. Navigation & Documentation

- **Help Key:** Use the Alpha keys to type a keyword (e.g., "G71") and press `F1`.
    
- **F2:** Standard "Back" or "Exit" key for most Haas menus.

### 💡 Personal Note on Tooling Stability:

You noted that "Lathe tools are bolted on and never change place." This is a huge advantage for you. In a Mill, you have to manage a "Tool Library" and "Pocket Map." On a Lathe, **Tool 1 is always in Station 1.** It makes the mental mapping of your G-Code much easier.
