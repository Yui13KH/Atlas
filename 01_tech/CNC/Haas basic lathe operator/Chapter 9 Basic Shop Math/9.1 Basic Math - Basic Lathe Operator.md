# 📏 Chapter 7.1.2: Units, Fractions, & Precision

### 1. The "Decimal Language" of Machinists

In a shop, we don't say "zero point zero zero one." We use specific slang to avoid confusion over the radio or in a loud shop:

|**Decimal**|**Written**|**Machinist Slang**|**Developer Logic**|
|---|---|---|---|
|**0.100**|`1/10`|**"One hundred thou"**|Large tolerance|
|**0.010**|`1/100`|**"Ten thou"**|Standard tolerance|
|**0.001**|`1/1000`|**"A thou"** (One thousandth)|High precision|
|**0.0001**|`1/10000`|**"A tenth"** (One ten-thousandth)|**Aerospace precision**|

> **⚠️ Warning:** Do not confuse "A tenth" ($0.0001"$) with "One-tenth of an inch" ($0.100"$). In a shop, "A tenth" always means the fourth decimal place.

### 2. Conversions (Fractional to Decimal)

Old-school blueprints often use fractions. As a programmer, you need decimals.

- **The Formula:** $Numerator \div Denominator = Decimal$
    
- **Common Shop Decimals:**
    
    - $1/8 = .125$
        
    - $1/4 = .250$
        
    - $3/8 = .375$
        
    - $1/2 = .500$
        

### 3. Metric vs. Imperial (The "Gold" Number)

Most CNC work is done in **Metric (mm)**, especially in Turkey and Germany.

- **The Constant:** $1" = 25.4\text{ mm}$
    
- **The Micron:** $1\text{ micron} = 0.001\text{ mm}$.
    
    - To put this in perspective: A human hair is about $50$ to $100$ microns thick. In high-end CNC, we often work within a $5\text{-}10$ micron tolerance.
        

### 4. Threads: The Two Systems

Threads are defined differently depending on the unit system:

- **Imperial (TPI - Threads Per Inch):** You count how many "peaks" fit into one inch.
    
    - _Example:_ $1/4\text{-}20$ means a $1/4"$ diameter bolt with $20$ threads per inch.
        
- **Metric (Pitch):** You measure the **distance** between two peaks.
    
    - _Example:_ $M6 \times 1.0$ means a $6\text{mm}$ diameter bolt with $1\text{mm}$ between each thread.
        

### 5. Measurement Tools

- **Tape Measure:** Good for raw material (stock). Accuracy: $\approx \pm 0.5\text{mm}$.
    
- **Scale (Steel Ruler):** Better for quick checks. Accuracy: $\approx \pm 0.2\text{mm}$.
    
- **Calipers:** Your daily driver. Accuracy: $\pm 0.02\text{mm}$.
    
- **Micrometers:** For critical tolerances. Accuracy: $\pm 0.002\text{mm}$.
