## 1. The Apostrophe ( Kesme İşareti : `'` )

**The MEB Rule:** You use it to separate suffixes from **Proper Nouns** (names, cities, countries, seas).

- _Examples:_ İstanbul’**un, Ahmet**’**e, Marmara**’yı.
    

> **⚠️ THE CRITICAL MEB TRAP:**
> 
> Do **NOT** use an apostrophe if the suffix is a **Plural** (`-ler / -lar`) or an **Institution/Organization** (schools, ministries, companies). MEB puts this trap in almost every spelling exam!

- _Wrong:_ Türk'ler (No! Must be: _Türkler_)
    
- _Wrong:_ İstanbul Üniversitesi'ne (No! Must be: _İstanbul Üniversitesine_)
    

## 2. The Comma ( Virgül : `,` )

**The MEB Rule:** Just like English, it separates items in a list, or links long sentences together.

- _Example:_ Pazardan elma, armut, muz aldım.
    

> **⚠️ THE CRITICAL MEB TRAP:**
> 
> You **cannot** put a comma after conditional suffixes like **-se / -sa** (if), or text linkers like **ve, veya, hem...hem...**.

- _Wrong:_ Erken gelirsen, sete gideriz. (The comma here is an instant error!)
    

## 3. The Semicolon ( Noktalı Virgül : `;` )

This is the boogeyman of Turkish exams, but its logic is actually very simple.

**The MEB Rule:** You **cannot** use a semicolon unless there is already a regular comma somewhere else in that sentence. A semicolon’s only job is to separate **different groups or categories** inside a big list.

- _Example:_ Erkek çocuklara Doğan, Tuğrul; kız çocuklara ise İnci, Çiçek ismi verilir.
    
    _(Notice how the commas separate the names, but the semicolon splits the **Boys group** from the **Girls group**)._
    

## 4. The Colon ( İki Nokta : `:` )

**The MEB Rule:** Used before an **explanation** or before giving a **list of examples**.

- _Example (List):_ En sevdiğim renkler şunlardır: mavi, kırmızı, yeşil.
    
- _Example (Explanation):_ Kendimi şöyle savundum: Ben o sırada gülmüyordum.
    

> **💡 MEB Pattern Hack:** If the text after the colon is a complete, standalone sentence, start it with a **Capital letter**. If it’s just a list of random words (like _mavi, kırmızı_), start it with a **lowercase letter**.

## Summary Checklist for Elimination

When looking at an exam sentence, look for these common rule breaks instantly:

1. Is there an apostrophe after a school/institution name? $\rightarrow$ **Error.**
    
2. Is there a comma right after an "-se/-sa" (if) clause? $\rightarrow$ **Error.**
    
3. Is there a semicolon in a sentence that doesn't have a single regular comma? $\rightarrow$ **Error.**

### NOTES 

### Option A) Servetifünun Edebiyatı (1896 – 1901)

- **The Vibe:** Extreme depression, escapism, and fancy, highly complex art.
    
- **The Story:** After Tanzimat's second period, the political environment became very oppressive. Writers could not talk about politics, society, or freedom at all. So, they gathered around a science/art magazine called _Servet-i Fünun_ (which literally means "Wealth of Sciences").
    
- **Key Patterns to look for in questions:**
    
    - **Extremely Heavy Language:** They completely abandoned simplification. They searched old dictionaries to find the most obscure Arabic and Persian words so they could write "fancy" poetry.
        
    - **Melancholy & Escapism:** Everything is sad, depressing, and dark. A very common theme is _hayal-hakikat çatışması_ (the clash between dreams and reality) and wanting to escape to a quiet island or New Zealand.
        
    - **Western Forms:** They fully mastered French poetic forms like **Sone** and **Terzarima**.
        

### Option C) Fecriati Edebiyatı (1909 – 1912)

- **The Vibe:** Highly dramatic, short-lived, and pretentious.
    
- **The Story:** After the Servetifünun group split up, a group of very young writers came together and published a manifesto (the first time in Turkish history a literary group published a formal declaration). Their name means "Dawn of the Future."
    
- **Key Patterns to look for in questions:**
    
    - **The Slogan:** They are famous for their signature slogan: _"Sanat şahsi ve muhteremdir"_ (**"Art is personal and respectable"**). If you see this quote in a question, the answer is instantly Fecriati!
        
    - **No Real Change:** Even though they criticized Servetifünun for being too old-fashioned, they ended up writing with the exact same heavy language and depressed themes.
        
    - **Short Lifespan:** They didn't agree on much, so the group broke up after only three years, with most members joining the Millî Edebiyat movement.
        

### Option D) Millî Edebiyat (1911 – 1923)

- **The Vibe:** Patriotic, plain Turkish, war-focused, and highly accessible.
    
- **The Story:** As the Ottoman Empire was collapsing, the Balkan Wars and WWI were happening. Writers realized that to save the country, they needed to unite the people. To do that, they had to write in a language that _everyone_ could understand.
    
- **Key Patterns to look for in questions:**
    
    - **Yeni Lisan (New Language):** This is their famous movement started in Izmir by Ömer Seyfettin and Ziya Gökalp. They completely threw out Arabic and Persian grammar rules and wrote in **pure, spoken Istanbul Turkish**.
        
    - **National Themes:** Instead of crying about love and death in fancy Istanbul mansions, they wrote about **Anatolia (Anadolu)**, the struggles of poor villagers, and the War of Independence (_Kurtuluş Savaşı_).
        
    - **Hece Vezni (Syllable Meter):** They stopped using the Arabic rhythmic poetic meter (_Aruz_) and started using the traditional Turkish folk meter (_Hece_) which is based on counting syllables.

### Vocabulary & Logic Argument: Where it went wrong

Your guess of B was a very logical, clever attempt based on the "characters" of the story (fruits vs. animals). However, this is where a **Turkish Literature Theory Trap** caught you.

#### 1. What is a Fable (Fabl)?

A fable is a short story teaching a moral lesson, where animals, plants, or objects talk. So fruits talking (Option B) is perfectly normal for a fable!

#### 2. The Sections of a Fable (Fablın Bölümleri):

Like a classic story, a fable has structured sections. MEB wants you to identify which section of the plot structure the passage represents:

1. **Serim (Introduction):** The characters are introduced, and the event begins. (Options A, B, and C are all starting a story. They set up the scene: a lion sleeping, fruits arguing, a thirsty crow looking at a pot).
    
2. **Düğüm (Development/Knot):** The conflict develops.
    
3. **Çözüm (Resolution):** The conflict is resolved.
    
4. **Öğüt (Moral/Conclusion):** The lesson of the story is explicitly stated, often by a character reflecting on their mistakes, or by the narrator.
    

Look closely at **Option D**. The deer is giving its last breath and explicitly declaring the moral lesson of the fable: _"This happened to me because I was ungrateful to the vine that protected me."_ This is the **Öğüt (Moral)** section, while A, B, and C are all **Serim (Introduction)** sections.

**Correct Answer: D**
