**1. Vocabulary Calibration
**
- **Kurmaca:** Not quite "fantasy" (_fantezi_), but **fiction**. It comes from the verb _kurmak_ (to build/construct). So _kurmaca_ is an invented or constructed narrative world (novels, stories).
    
- **Eser:** **Work / Piece of art** (book, painting, song, etc.).
    
- **Roman vs. Hikâye:** _Roman_ is a **novel** (long fiction), while _hikâye_ is a **short story**.
    
- **Kurgulamak:** **To plot / to structure fiction** (creating the setup, characters, and events).
    

**2. Breakdown of the Options & Elimination Logic**

  

- **A) Eserlerinizin türünü belirlerken nelere dikkat ediyorsunuz?**
    
    _(What do you pay attention to when determining the genre of your works?)_
    
    - **Why it's wrong:** The author explicitly says _"fiction is fiction"_ (_kurmaca, kurmacadır_) regardless of genre. The focus isn't on choosing genres like poetry vs. novel.
        
- **B) Yazarken hitap ettiğiniz yaş grubunu dikkate alıyor musunuz?**
    
    _(Do you take into account the age group you are addressing when writing?)_

    - **Why it's correct:** The entire paragraph directly contrasts writing for **youths** (_gençler_) versus **children** (_çocuklar_). The author explains how their language tone (_sert, karmaşık_ vs. _umut dolu_) shifts based on the target age group.
        
- **C) Roman ve hikâye kurgulamak sizce zor mudur?**
    
    _(Do you think plotting novels and short stories is difficult?)_
    
    - **Why it's wrong:** The author never mentions whether writing is easy or hard (_zor/kolay_).
        
- **D) Yetişkinlerle çocukların hayal dünyaları farklı mıdır?**
    
    _(Are the imaginary worlds of adults and children different?)_
    
    - **Why it's wrong:** This asks about the **readers' imagination** (_hayal dünyaları_). The passage focuses strictly on the **author's writing style** (_kalemimi oynatıyorum, yazma imkânı yakalıyorum_), not what goes on inside the readers' heads.
